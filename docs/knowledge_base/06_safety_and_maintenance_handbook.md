# Aravalli Solar Works — Safety and Maintenance Handbook

## Panel Cleaning

### How often

- Normal areas: once every 15 days.
- Dusty areas, near construction sites or unpaved roads: once a week.
- Monsoon season: rain usually cleans the panels; inspect once a month and remove leaves or bird droppings.

### How to clean

- Clean early in the morning before 8:00 AM or in the evening after 6:00 PM. Never pour water on hot panels, because the sudden temperature change can crack the glass.
- Use plain water with a soft sponge or a soft-bristle brush.
- Do not use detergent, soap, acid cleaners, hard water with heavy scaling, or a pressure washer.
- Do not stand, sit or place buckets on the panels.
- Do not clean during rain, strong wind or when the roof is slippery.

### What customers must not do

Customers must not open the inverter, the DC box, the AC box or any cable connector. Customers must not move panels, add panels or connect any other equipment to the solar wiring.

## Field Engineer Safety Rules

1. Wear a helmet, insulated gloves, safety shoes and eye protection on every roof job.
2. Use a full-body harness with an anchored lifeline on sloped roofs and on any roof edge without a parapet of at least 1 metre.
3. Work in pairs. No engineer works alone on a roof.
4. Treat DC cables as live at all times during daylight. Cover panels with an opaque sheet when DC work is required.
5. Use lock-out and tag-out on the AC isolator and the solar MCB before opening any enclosure.
6. Use only insulated tools rated for 1,000 volts.
7. Stop work if lightning is seen or thunder is heard, and wait at least 30 minutes after the last thunder.
8. Stop roof work when wind makes it difficult to handle a panel safely.
9. Report every near miss in the field app on the same day.

## Preventive Maintenance Visit Checklist

A maintenance visit under any Care plan includes:

- Clean panels and check for cracks, hot spots, discolouration and snail trails.
- Check the mounting structure for loose bolts and corrosion.
- Inspect cable conduits, glands and MC4 connectors for heat damage or water entry.
- Measure earthing resistance at all three pits. Add water and earthing compound if the reading is above 5 ohms.
- Check surge protection devices. Replace any that show a red indicator.
- Clean the inverter air vents and confirm the fan is running.
- Download the inverter event log and review repeated codes.
- Confirm the HelioBox gateway is online and the firmware is current.

CarePlus and CareTotal visits also include a string voltage and current test and a thermal camera scan of panels and connections.

## After a Storm or Heavy Rain

Customers should look at the system from a safe distance. If panels have moved, glass is broken, cables are hanging or water is seen inside a box, they should follow the emergency shutdown card and call the emergency line. Field Service inspects storm-affected systems free of charge within 3 working days, although any storm damage repair is chargeable or claimed through the customer's insurance.

## Monkeys, Birds and Rodents

- Bird nests under panels should be removed during maintenance visits; a bird mesh can be fitted as a paid add-on.
- In areas with monkeys, the optional mesh guard and conduit covers are strongly recommended at installation time.
- Chewed cables must never be taped by the customer. Raise a P2 ticket.

## Fire Safety

A dry powder or carbon dioxide extinguisher should be kept near the inverter. Water must not be used on electrical equipment. In case of fire, the customer should leave the area, call the fire service first, and then call the Aravalli emergency line.
